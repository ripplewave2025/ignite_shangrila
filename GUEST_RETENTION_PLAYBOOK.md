# Ignite guest retention playbook

## The principle

Do not sell “customer-data collection.” Sell **remembering guests—with permission—so every return feels warmer**.

Use two names:

- **Ignite Inner Circle** — the optional guest community.
- **Secret Sessions** — invitation-only music and listening events.

Keep three records separate:

1. **Reservation contact:** used only to manage one booking.
2. **One-night guest list:** used only for that event/check-in.
3. **Ongoing membership/marketing:** requires a separate, affirmative choice.

For the first 30-day pilot, keep the record deliberately small: preferred name, chosen contact channel, permission record, visit count, and optional music or seating preference. Defer spend bands, no-show profiling and detailed preference history until the team proves that the smaller system is useful and responsibly maintained.

## What to collect

### Required for a reservation

- First/preferred name.
- Mobile number.
- Visit date/time.
- Party size.

### Optional, guest-supplied preferences

- Email and preferred contact channel.
- Birthday day/month, not necessarily full date of birth.
- Local or visitor city.
- Occasion.
- Music genres and up to three artists/songs.
- Preferred zone: dance floor, social, lounge or quieter table.
- Food or private-party interest.
- Service-only allergy or accessibility needs, held outside the marketing profile with narrower staff access. Reconfirm allergy information on every visit rather than trusting an old note.

### System-derived, restricted fields

- Last visit and visit count.
- Typical party size and visit time.
- Aggregate spend band, not payment details.
- Events attended and booking source.
- Cancellations/no-shows.
- Objective feedback and service resolution.

Never store card/UPI details, ID copies, companions' numbers, scraped social profiles, gossip, caste, religion, politics or intimate relationship information. Keep safety incidents separate and tightly controlled. DJs and promoters never receive spend data or full guest profiles.

## Ethical recognition levels

- **New:** first visit.
- **Returning:** second visit.
- **Regular:** three or more visits within 90 days.
- **House Guest:** six visits within 90 days or a manager-recognized community/event host—not spending alone.
- **Celebration Host:** repeat birthday, group or private-event organizer.
- **Lapsed:** no visit for 45–60 days.

Possible benefits:

- Name recognition and preferred area when available.
- Priority waitlist or 48-hour event access.
- Birthday booking priority.
- Secret Sessions invitations.
- An occasional food, dessert or non-alcohol surprise.
- Optional song dedication.

Status never overrides safety, entry rules, payment or responsible service.

## The favorite-song idea

Ask:

> What three songs or artists make your night?

Before an event, generate an anonymous DJ brief showing genre demand and suitable requests. Share a guest's name/table only when that guest explicitly asks for a dedication. Promise only: **“We’ll try to include it when it fits tonight’s set.”** The DJ always keeps creative control.

## Useful retention journeys

1. **Booking:** immediate confirmation with map, entry rules and one-click change/cancel; reminder 24 hours before.
2. **First visit +1 day:** named thank-you and private three-question feedback.
3. **Poor feedback:** manager task within 24 hours; pause promotions until resolved.
4. **Second visit:** quiet “welcome back” using useful preferences only.
5. **Birthday:** reservation-priority invitation ten days before, if opted in.
6. **Event fan:** invite only matching genre/music interests.
7. **Lapsed guest:** one relevant event after 45–60 days, not a generic discount blast.
8. **Cancellation:** easy rebooking, not pressure.
9. **Celebration host:** relevant private-event options when appropriate.

Start with at most two promotional emails monthly and no more than one separately opted-in WhatsApp event update weekly. Track opt-outs and blocks as health signals.

## Suggested permission wording

Reservation:

> Use my mobile number to confirm and manage this reservation.

Separate, unticked email choice:

> Join Ignite Inner Circle. Email me selected music and event invitations from Ignite by Shangri-La, around 2–4 per month. Unsubscribe anytime.

Separate, unticked WhatsApp choice:

> Also send me up to one WhatsApp update a week about live music, DJs and table availability from Ignite by Shangri-La. Reply STOP anytime.

Separate, unticked SMS choice:

> Send me up to two SMS event invitations a month from Ignite by Shangri-La. Reply STOP anytime.

Separate, unticked telephone choice:

> Ignite may call me about private-event or celebration enquiries I ask about. Do not use this permission for routine promotional calling.

Separate personalization choice:

> Remember the preferences I choose to share and my visit history so future visits can feel more personal.

A booking must work when every marketing choice is declined. Record wording version, source, channel, timestamp and withdrawal.

One-night guest-list notice:

> Use my name and arrival details only to manage check-in for this event. Ignite and its authorized check-in team may see the list; delete it after the event according to the stated retention period. This does not join me to marketing.

Before launch, publish a complete notice naming the legal business, each data purpose, processors/tools, retention periods, privacy contact and withdrawal/deletion route. The short checkbox wording links to that notice; it does not replace it.

## Deletion and responsibility

The owner must approve exact periods before launch. A practical starting proposal is:

- Delete unused one-night guest lists within 7 days after reconciliation.
- Delete unconfirmed enquiries after 30 days unless the person asked for follow-up.
- Review inactive Inner Circle profiles after 12 months; ask to renew permission or delete them.
- Process STOP/unsubscribe immediately and preserve only a minimal suppression record so the person is not contacted again.
- Keep invoices or legally required financial records in the accounting system, separate from marketing profiles, for the period the venue's accountant advises.

Name one owner-side privacy contact and one backup to handle access, correction, unsubscribe and deletion requests. DJs, promoters and NexGen should receive only the minimum event data needed and must delete their copies on schedule.

For crowd photography, collect identifiable-image permission separately, obtain staff/model releases, provide a visible takedown contact and never assume entry equals promotional consent.

## What leading venues and groups already do

- [Ministry of Sound](https://ministryofsound.com/tickets/) and [The Piano Man](https://www.thepianoman.in/venue/event/list/3/4/0) give events their own pages with dates, genres, artist information and booking.
- [Hakkasan](https://taogroup.com/venues/hakkasan-nightclub-las-vegas/) separates tickets, VIP tables and special-event enquiries rather than hiding everything inside DMs.
- [TAO Rewards](https://taogroup.com/rewards/) connects reservations, event discovery and rewards across nightlife and dining.
- [Hyatt Dining Club India](https://www.hyattdiningclub.com/) demonstrates that tiered dining memberships are familiar in India, although Ignite should start free and simple.
- [Hard Rock](https://cafe.hardrock.com/parties-and-events-request.aspx) uses a structured private-event enquiry rather than “contact us” alone.
- [ATLAS Singapore's Juniper Society](https://atlasbar.sg/juniper-society) builds loyalty through community, learning and events rather than discounts alone.
- [Lyaness London](https://www.lyaness.com/) combines reservations, programming, private events, preference-led discovery and a newsletter in one journey.
- [SevenRooms for nightclubs and bars](https://sevenrooms.com/nightclubs-bars/) and [OpenTable relationship management](https://www.opentable.com/restaurant-solutions/products/relationship-management/) show how visits, preferences, occasions and feedback can prepare staff before service.
- [Rockbot Request](https://rockbot.com/request) and [TouchTunes for bars](https://business.touchtunes.com/) demonstrate controlled guest music requests. Availability and licensing in India must be checked; Ignite can begin with its own genre poll and request form.

These examples prove the patterns, not guaranteed results for Ignite.

## What guests tend to value

Directional hospitality research suggests that guests value:

- Simple rewards and easy booking.
- Birthday/anniversary recognition.
- Early access to unique events.
- Personalization they understand and control.
- Human recognition more than complicated points.
- Experiences such as live music, dance and private programming.

Sources: [SevenRooms 2025 US research](https://sevenrooms.com/press/2025-US-data-report/) and [Deloitte 2025 restaurant report](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone3/us/en/img/primary/full/industries/consumer/2024/deloitte-2025-future-of-restaurants-and-food-service.pdf). These audiences are not Darjeeling; validate priorities with actual Ignite guests.

## Measure preservation, not vanity

- First-to-second-visit rate within 60 days.
- Returning guests ÷ identified guests.
- Visits per active member and 30/60/90-day cohorts.
- Campaign reservations, actual seated guests and revenue through unique links/codes.
- Lapsed guests who return ÷ lapsed guests contacted.
- No-show and cancellation rates.
- Feedback response time and issues closed within 24 hours.
- Opt-out, WhatsApp block/report and complaint rates.

Set targets after the first 30-day baseline.

## Messaging and privacy guardrails

- WhatsApp requires the person to provide their number and opt in; opt-outs must be honored. Keep proposed messaging to reservations, music, events, food and tables—not alcohol products, prices or offers. [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy?lang=en_US)
- Bulk commercial SMS/calls require the proper TRAI sender, consent and template processes. Do not use personal SIMs for bulk calling. [TRAI advice to senders](https://www.trai.gov.in/advice-to-senders)
- India’s DPDP framework is being phased in. Build now for plain-language purpose notices, affirmative choice, data minimization, security, access control, easy withdrawal and deletion. [DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf) and [DPDP Rules 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf).
- Do not build nightlife marketing profiles for minors. Keep door-level ID/age checks separate from the CRM.
- Use owner-controlled accounts, two named administrators, 2FA, role-based access and a real system—not a guest spreadsheet passed through staff WhatsApp groups.

This is product/operations guidance, not legal advice. Re-check laws and platform policies before launch.
