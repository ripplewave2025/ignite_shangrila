# Saturday visit — Ignite by Shangri-La

## The mission

Do not pitch posting. Say:

> Your children can handle posting. I want to understand whether I can build the system underneath it—website, reservations, guest memory and follow-up—so your team owns it.

The goal is permission for one small pilot, not a full commitment.

## The 12 questions that matter on Saturday

Arrange this conversation **before opening**. Keep it to 25–30 minutes, then return later only to observe the guest journey without occupying the manager.

1. [ ] Which result matters first: filling a weak night, repeat visits, private parties or fewer missed bookings?
2. [ ] Which night is weakest, and what would a good night look like in actual seated guests?
3. [ ] Why do regulars return, and what do guests complain about most?
4. [ ] Where do reservations arrive now, and which channel loses enquiries?
5. [ ] Do you collect names, phones or emails—and did those guests consent only to the booking or also to future messages?
6. [ ] Who owns and answers the official phone, WhatsApp, Instagram, Google profile and email?
7. [ ] What happened at the last three events: enquiries, reservations, arrivals, no-shows, average bill range and total event cost?
8. [ ] Who is the one internal system captain who can maintain this for 30 minutes twice a week?
9. [ ] What can the children already do well, and what operational work is currently falling through the gap?
10. [ ] Which guest details would genuinely improve service, and which details should Ignite never keep?
11. [ ] What are the real capacity, entry, safety, closing-hour and music/event licence limits?
12. [ ] If we test one event page plus one booking flow for 30 days, who approves it and what number will decide whether it worked?

## Suggested two-part visit

- [ ] **Before opening · 0–5 minutes:** Meet the decision-maker and ask permission to take notes.
- [ ] **Before opening · 5–30 minutes:** Ask the 12 essential questions above.
- [ ] **Before opening · 30–40 minutes:** Show only the three prototype moments and agree on one next step.
- [ ] **During service · 30–45 minutes:** Observe the guest journey without interrupting the team or collecting guest data.
- [ ] **Follow-up meeting:** Use the detailed questions below only where the first conversation exposed a gap.

## Ask the owner or manager

### Outcome and customers

- [ ] Which result matters most: weak-night turnout, repeat visits, private parties, tourist discovery or event profit?
- [ ] Who are the best-fit guests: locals, tourists, couples, groups, hotel guests or companies?
- [ ] Why do regulars return? What do guests complain about most?
- [ ] Which days and hours are strongest and weakest?
- [ ] What do valued guests appreciate most: recognition, privacy, preferred table, music, speed, food consistency or celebration help?
- [ ] What must never change about the Ignite experience?

### Reservations and retention

- [ ] Where do bookings arrive: call, WhatsApp, Instagram, Google, promoter or walk-in?
- [ ] Who answers each channel, and how many enquiries are missed?
- [ ] Do you already collect names, phone numbers or emails? Where are they stored?
- [ ] Did those guests agree to future marketing, or only to manage that booking?
- [ ] Which POS/reservation system is used? Can it export visit date, party size and bill totals without payment data?
- [ ] Do staff record birthdays, preferred zones, genres, songs, food preferences or accessibility needs?
- [ ] What happens after a guest leaves: thank-you, feedback, review request, future invitation or nothing?
- [ ] Who owns the contacts if a DJ, promoter or employee leaves?

**Important:** a booking phone number is not automatically marketing permission.

### Last three events

For each event, ask for:

- [ ] Capacity, reservations, walk-ins, no-shows and actual attendance.
- [ ] Cover/minimum spend and average bill range.
- [ ] DJ, artist, security, production, advertising, promoter and complimentary-table costs.
- [ ] Break-even number of guests.
- [ ] How guests heard about it.
- [ ] Enquiries → reservations → arrivals.
- [ ] Which part generated value: entry, food, tables, private parties or another source?

Do not judge an event by Reel views. Judge it by attributable guests and event profit.

### Staff and ownership

- [ ] Who will be the internal system captain?
- [ ] What can the children already handle well?
- [ ] Who captures footage and who approves content?
- [ ] Can one person spend 30 minutes twice weekly maintaining events and replies?
- [ ] Which languages should the site and messages support?
- [ ] Who may see aggregate spend bands and guest history?
- [ ] What happens when the system captain is absent?

Inventory these owner-controlled accounts:

- [ ] Google Business Profile.
- [ ] Instagram, Facebook and Meta Business Suite.
- [ ] WhatsApp Business.
- [ ] Business email.
- [ ] POS/reservations.
- [ ] Domain and DNS.
- [ ] Website/hosting.
- [ ] Analytics and advertising.

Ask for role-based access later. Never request passwords, copy OTPs or take ownership of their accounts.

On Saturday, do **not** inspect, photograph or export live guest records. Ask for a blank screen/demo, a field list or aggregate counts unless the legal owner has explicitly arranged controlled access. Do not put real customer data on a NexGen device during discovery.

## Walk the customer journey

- [ ] Can a new visitor find tonight's correct event, time and map pin?
- [ ] Can they understand music, entry, price and table availability?
- [ ] How fast is a call or DM answered?
- [ ] Is the confirmation clear about date, arrival and cancellation?
- [ ] Is the entrance obvious from Nehru Road/Chowrasta?
- [ ] Are returning guests recognized naturally?
- [ ] Are menu, ordering, billing and payment clear?
- [ ] Is there a respectful way to request music?
- [ ] Are security, queues, washrooms, lighting and women’s comfort taken seriously?
- [ ] Who handles harassment, intoxicated guests, medical incidents and safe transport home—and how is an incident escalated?
- [ ] Are occupancy/fire limits, evacuation routes, security responsibility, closing permissions and music/event licences current and understood?
- [ ] Is feedback invited without review-gating?
- [ ] What gives a first-time guest a reason to return?

Observe mobile signal, queue handoffs, signage, staff briefing, sound zones, photo lighting and payment flow. Do not photograph identifiable guests without their permission. A general venue-entry notice should never silently become consent for marketing images; ask who collects releases and who handles takedown requests.

## Facts and assets required for the website

- [ ] Brand/legal name and relationship among the Shangri-La entities.
- [ ] Address, floor, map pin, official phone, WhatsApp and hours.
- [ ] Logo files and brand ownership approval.
- [ ] Menu and prices.
- [ ] Capacity and available areas.
- [ ] Reservation, deposit, cancellation and no-show rules.
- [ ] Entry/age/ID, dress, cover and last-entry rules.
- [ ] Parking/taxi landmark, accessibility and safety information.
- [ ] Next three events with artists, times, pricing and approved images.
- [ ] Approved interior, stage, food, team and crowd photographs.
- [ ] Private-event formats and operational limits.
- [ ] Privacy contact and nominated system administrator.
- [ ] Domain preference and legal owner.

## Show only these three things

1. The mobile `Tonight at Ignite` event journey.
2. Guest list/table/private-event reservation choices.
3. `Ignite Inner Circle`: optional, permission-based guest memory.

## Close with one question

> If I return with this mobile website, one real event-booking flow and the Inner Circle form connected as a small pilot, who approves it and when can we review it for twenty minutes?
